Working Papers

A Separation of Uncertainty Attitudes and Belief Updating

This paper proposes a natural and tractable updating rule for Choquet expected utility with probabilistic sophistication, grounded in Bayesian updating. A probabilistically sophisticated decision maker expresses beliefs by a subjective probability measure, which serves as a Bayesian prior and is updated to a posterior according to Bayes’ rule when new information becomes available. Under certain conditions, a non-additive capacity can be represented as a transformation of an additive probability measure. Leveraging this representation, beliefs are updated by applying Bayes’ rule to the underlying probability measure, with the updated capacity derived by applying the same transformation to posterior probabilities. We provide an axiomatisation of this updating procedure and develop a simple and intuitive updating rule that neatly separates beliefs from attitudes towards uncertainty, with beliefs evolving in a Bayesian manner and uncertainty attitudes remaining unchanged.

Dynamic Preference Foundations of Expected Dichotomic Discounting

We study dynamic preferences over lotteries in which both outcomes and their occurring timing are uncertain, referred to as timed risks. This chapter examines how present-biased, time-inconsistent decision makers evaluate such timed risks in dynamic settings. We combine dichotomic discounting, a continuous-time extension of quasi-hyperbolic discounting, and expected exponential discounting, the most extensively used model for risky choice over time. This yields a new model, expected dichotomic discounting, in which time is divided into two pieces: exponential discounting applies within each time period but not necessarily across both periods. The model extends continuous-time quasi-hyperbolic discounting to risky contexts and provides a more robust approach for modelling present-biased preferences under risk. Based on an axiomatic analysis, this study also provides dynamic preference foundations for our brand-new model, as applied to dynamic choice over timed risks in particular.

Preference Foundations of Sign-Dependent Exponential Discounting

We develop an axiomatic foundation for a model that integrates sign-dependent preferences into the standard framework of exponential discounting over time. We propose a novel formulation in which individuals evaluate gains and losses asymmetrically across temporal sequences. We identify and formalise the minimal set of axioms required to represent such preferences and examine their implications for intertemporal choice. The resulting framework, which we term sign-dependent exponential discounting, provides a tractable yet behaviourally richer alternative to conventional models.
