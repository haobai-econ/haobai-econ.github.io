A Separation of Uncertainty Attitudes and Belief Updating

This chapter proposes a natural and tractable updating rule for Choquet expected utility with probabilistic sophistication, grounded in Bayesian updating. A probabilistically sophisticated decision maker expresses beliefs by a subjective probability measure, which serves as a Bayesian prior and is updated to a posterior according to Bayes’ rule when new information becomes available. Under certain conditions, a non-additive capacity can be represented as a transformation of an additive probability measure. Leveraging this representation, beliefs are updated by applying Bayes’ rule to the underlying probability measure, with the updated capacity derived by applying the same transformation to posterior probabilities. We provide an axiomatisation of this updating procedure and develop a simple and intuitive updating rule that neatly separates beliefs from attitudes towards uncertainty, with beliefs evolving in a Bayesian manner and uncertainty attitudes remaining unchanged.

Dynamic Preference Foundations of Expected Dichotomic Discounting

This paper studies intertemporal choice under risk and develops dynamic preference foundations for expected dichotomic discounting. The model allows different exponential discount factors to apply over short and long time intervals, separated by a threshold. This structure provides a tractable way to accommodate departures from standard exponential discounting while preserving important forms of dynamic consistency.

Sign-Dependent Exponential Discounting

This paper develops an axiomatic foundation for a model of sign-dependent exponential discounting. The model allows the discounting of dated outcomes to depend on whether outcomes are perceived as gains or losses relative to a reference point. Separate discount factors for gains and losses provide a flexible framework for studying intertemporal choice while retaining the tractability of exponential discounting.
